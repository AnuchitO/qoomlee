# QOOMLEE AIRLINE
## Route Map & Market Analysis — Asia–Australia Network
**Confidential Working Document | Version 1.0 | May 2026**

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Qoomlee is a proposed hybrid (LCC comfort+) carrier headquartered in Kalasin Province, northeastern Thailand. It aims to serve an underconnected corridor stretching from Tier-2 cities in Southeast Asia to key Australian gateways. The timing is strong: Asia-Pacific air passenger demand grew 9.3% year-on-year as of late 2025, the highest growth rate of any region globally, and passenger numbers in Asia-Pacific are projected to double by 2043.

Qoomlee's core proposition: **affordable fares with a noticeably better experience than pure LCCs** — targeting the fast-growing middle-income traveller in Southeast Asia who is unwilling to pay full-service prices but frustrated by bare-bones budget travel.

## 1. STRATEGIC CONTEXT

### 1.1 The Market Opportunity

- **Asia-Pacific holds 33.5% of global passenger traffic share** (by RPK, 2024) and is growing faster than any other region.
- Asia-Pacific international traffic grew **8.0% in 2025**, outpacing global RPK growth of 6.8%.
- **600+ new routes** have launched across Asia-Pacific since 2015, yet secondary and Tier-2 city connectivity remains severely underserved.
- The Australia aviation market is valued at USD 19.6 billion (2024) and projected to reach **USD 29.4 billion by 2032** (CAGR 5.05%).
- Southeast Asia is identified as a critical emerging growth region alongside India, driven by rising middle-class income and affordable smartphone-led booking behaviour.

### 1.2 Kalasin as HQ: The Strategic Case

Kalasin is a bold, first-mover choice. Here's why it works:

| Factor | Analysis |
|---|---|
| **Airport** | Sarasin Airport (planned): feasibility study complete, 2B THB budget, serves both Kalasin & Maha Sarakham |
| **Government backing** | PM-level endorsement; part of Thailand's DOA plan to invest 50B THB in 7 new regional airports |
| **Competition** | Zero — no commercial airline is based here |
| **Labour cost** | Significantly below Bangkok, Chiang Mai, or any Australian/Singaporean base |
| **Land & infrastructure** | Abundant, low-cost land for MRO, training, admin HQ |
| **Risk** | Sarasin Airport timeline is 2027–2028 at earliest; interim ops must route through Khon Kaen (KKC, ~80km away) |

**Interim HQ strategy:** Establish Qoomlee's legal entity and operations base at Khon Kaen International Airport while Sarasin Airport is completed. Plan for HQ relocation to Kalasin by 2028–2029.

## 2. NETWORK DESIGN

### 2.1 Network Philosophy

Qoomlee does NOT try to compete on Bangkok–Sydney or Kuala Lumpur–Melbourne mega-routes. Instead, it pursues a **point-to-point thin-route strategy**, connecting:

- **Underserved Thai & Mekong regional cities** → **Australian gateways**
- **Secondary Southeast Asian cities** → **Australian secondary cities**

This mirrors the early success of AirAsia (KL-based) and Cebu Pacific (Manila-based) but focuses on the **Mekong/Indochina + Australia** corridor, which has almost no dedicated carrier.

### 2.2 Phase 1 Routes — Launch Network (Year 1–2)
*Hub-and-spoke from Khon Kaen (KKC)*

| Route | Distance | Type | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Khon Kaen (KKC) → Bangkok Suvarnabhumi (BKK) | 445 km | Domestic feeder | Connect Northeast Thailand to international hub |
| Khon Kaen (KKC) → Vientiane (VTE) | 190 km | Short-haul intl | Laos–Thai cross-border; underserved |
| Bangkok (BKK) → Perth (PER) | 7,200 km | Long-haul trunk | Perth is fastest-growing Australian intl airport (+12.5% in 2025) |
| Bangkok (BKK) → Melbourne (MEL) | 7,500 km | Long-haul trunk | Melbourne handled 37M pax in 2025, record high; strong SEA diaspora |

**Phase 1 Fleet Requirement:** 2–3 narrow/medium-body aircraft for domestic/regional; 2 widebody aircraft (e.g. A330-900neo or B787-9) for Australia trunk routes.

### 2.3 Phase 2 Routes — Network Expansion (Year 3–4)
*Adding spokes from Bangkok and launching Kalasin/Sarasin Airport*

| Route | Distance | Type | Rationale |

| Chiang Mai (CNX) → Sydney (SYD) | 7,900 km | Long-haul | Large Thai diaspora in Sydney; zero nonstop options today |

| Phnom Penh (PNH) → Perth (PER) | 5,700 km | Long-haul | Cambodia–Australia corridor; growing but underserved |

## 7. REGULATORY ROADMAP

### 7.1 Key Licenses Required

| License | Issuing Body | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Air Operator Certificate (AOC) — Thailand | Civil Aviation Authority of Thailand (CAAT) | 18–24 months |
| Foreign Airline License — Australia | CASA (Civil Aviation Safety Authority) | 12–18 months |
| IATA membership | IATA | Apply post-AOC |
| Traffic rights (5th freedom) | Bilateral Air Services Agreements | Negotiate per country pair |
| Slot allocations — BKK, PER, MEL | Airport Coordination | 12–24 months ahead of launch |

### 7.2 Thailand Open Skies Context
Thailand is an ASEAN Open Skies signatory, which simplifies intra-ASEAN routes significantly. The Thailand–Australia bilateral air services agreement (ASA) is in place, providing the legal framework for Qoomlee's Australia routes. Australia's CASA has a defined foreign carrier licensing process averaging 12–18 months.
